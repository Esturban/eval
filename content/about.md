EV Advisory exists for one reason: to tell a team the truth about whether something is worth building, buying, or trusting, before they spend the money.

When an AI idea sounds good in the pitch, the sales deck, or the vendor proposal, teams still slow down. Budget decisions get harder to defend. Competitive threats stay fuzzy. Governance becomes a scramble instead of a routine check.

That is the gap EV Advisory is built to close.

## What EV Advisory does

EV Advisory helps teams answer a costly question before they spend the budget. Most engagements begin in one of these places:

- an AI idea an owner-operator is not sure is narrow enough to build
- a competitive pattern a sales leader cannot yet turn into a fixable list
- a governance or security question a procurement or IT lead needs answered before they sign
- for ecommerce and Shopify teams specifically, reporting trust, attribution noise, conversion friction, or operational drag

The work is diagnostic first. The goal is to make the decision easier to trust, easier to explain, and easier to act on.

## Who leads the work

EV Advisory is led by **Esteban Valencia**, a data and growth operator who works across strategy, implementation, and operating design.

The practice is intentionally narrow. It is not trying to be a general-purpose marketing agency. The focus is on the layer where teams lose confidence before they commit budget:

- the relationship between measurement and decision-making
- the relationship between site friction and commercial performance
- the relationship between reporting quality and operating speed

## How the work is approached

EV Advisory does not start by adding more tools. It starts by identifying where confidence breaks.

That usually means:

1. clarifying the real decision the team is trying to make
2. tracing where the supporting evidence becomes noisy or contradictory
3. identifying the highest-risk gaps, whether that is scope, competitive blind spots, or governance controls
4. turning those findings into a decision the team can act on with confidence

The output should feel practical. Teams should end up with fewer unresolved arguments, not more documentation.

## Why teams hire EV Advisory

The value is usually not that data or documentation suddenly exists. The value is that a decision becomes easier once the noise is reduced.

Teams tend to reach out when:

- an AI idea keeps getting re-pitched because nobody has scoped whether it is narrow enough to work
- sales keeps losing the same deals and nobody has turned the pattern into a fixable list
- procurement or IT will not move until governance and security questions have real answers
- reporting or operations work is still manual enough to steal time from the real decision
