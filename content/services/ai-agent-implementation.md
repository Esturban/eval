EV Advisory implements AI agents for founder-led agencies and service teams in Canada and the United States: one narrow agent, built into the tools you already use, tested on your real work, and handed over so your existing team can run it without a new hire. Every project starts with a free 20-minute [Live Audit](/live-audit/), where Esteban Valencia reviews your actual tool stack with you and names the first process worth handing to an agent.

The work is deliberately narrow. One agent, one repeated process, one owner on your side, and a clear measure of whether it worked. A second agent only gets scoped once the first one is earning its keep.

*Updated 2026-09-24. Written by Esteban Valencia, founder of EV Advisory.*

## Who is this for?

This service fits if all three of these are true:

- **You run a founder-led agency or service team.** Marketing, creative, consulting, or professional services, where the founder still feels every bottleneck directly.
- **Your team already works in real tools.** A CRM, a project or ticketing tool, shared inboxes, a document system. An agent needs somewhere to plug in.
- **You are open to adopting an agent.** Someone on the team is willing to own the process, review the agent's work in the first weeks, and change a habit or two.

It is not a fit, yet, if:

- The process you want automated is not written down anywhere and changes every time it runs.
- You want a large platform rollout across many departments at once. That needs a bigger team than a senior-led practice.
- You need the agent to make final decisions on money, hiring, legal, or medical matters without a person checking. That is not something this practice builds.

If you are unsure, the [Live Audit](/live-audit/) exists to answer exactly that question in 20 minutes.

## How long does it take?

A single narrow agent is usually live within **2 to 6 weeks** of kickoff. The exact dates are set in your written scope.

| Phase | What happens | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|
| Live Audit | Free call on your screen. We walk through your tools and name the first process worth automating. | 20 minutes |
| Scope | Written scope: the process, the tools involved, what the agent does, what stays human, how success is measured, and the quote. | 2 to 5 business days |
| Access and examples | Your team grants tool access and shares real past examples of the work. | Usually 2 to 5 business days (this is the step that most often slows projects down) |
| Build | The agent is built inside your accounts and connected to your tools. | 1 to 3 weeks |
| Test on real work | The agent runs against your past examples, then alongside your team on live work, with a person approving every output. | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Launch and handoff | The agent moves into daily use. Your owner gets the runbook, a walkthrough, and the access list. | 1 to 3 days |
| Early monitoring | We watch exceptions and error rates and tune the agent while the team settles in. | First 2 to 4 weeks after launch |

## What does your team need to do?

An agent only works if someone on your side owns it. During the project your team:

- **Names one process owner.** The person who does the work today, or manages it. They answer questions and approve outputs.
- **Grants access.** To the specific tools named in the scope, using accounts your business owns. Access is scoped to the minimum the agent needs.
- **Shares real examples.** Usually 20 to 30 past instances of the work (emails, briefs, tickets, reports) so the agent is tested on reality, not a demo.
- **Reviews at checkpoints.** Two or three agreed review points: after the scope, after testing on past examples, and before launch.
- **Plans for one to two hours a week** from the owner during the build and test phases.

## How much does it cost?

Each project is quoted in writing after the free Live Audit, once the process, the tools involved, and the amount of judgment the task needs are clear. There is no charge for the Live Audit and no obligation after it.

What moves the quote most:

- **Number of tools the agent connects to.** One tool is simpler than four.
- **How much judgment the task needs.** Sorting and drafting is simpler than deciding.
- **Whether the agent touches client or customer data.** More safeguards, more testing.
- **Whether a person approves every output or only exceptions.**
