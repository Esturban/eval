**You can set up your first AI agent yourself if it lives inside one tool, touches no customer or client data, and you can describe the workflow in one sentence. Hire help when the agent has to connect two or more systems, act on customer data, or run without a person checking each output.**

Most founders sit somewhere between those two lines. The decision table below sorts the common situations, and the section after it covers the part DIY advice usually leaves out: what it costs in your own time.

*Updated 2026-09-24 by Esteban Valencia, EV Advisory. Written for founder-led businesses in Canada and the United States.*

## When can you set up AI agents yourself?

Do it yourself when the work is personal or internal and the blast radius of a mistake is small. Good DIY starting points:

- **Your own inbox and calendar.** Drafting replies, summarising threads, prepping for meetings.
- **Writing and research assistance.** First drafts, outlines, summaries of documents you already have.
- **A single built-in agent inside a tool you already pay for.** Many CRMs, help desks, and project tools now ship agent features you can switch on and configure.
- **A simple two-step automation** in a no-code tool, where a person still approves the result.

If you can test the whole thing yourself in an afternoon, and nobody outside the business sees the output unreviewed, DIY is the right call.

## When should you hire help?

Hire help when any of these are true:

- **The agent connects two or more systems**, for example reading from your project tool and writing to your CRM. Integrations are where most of the effort and most of the breakage lives.
- **It acts on customer or client personal information.** Canada's privacy regulators expect businesses using AI to have a legal basis for the data, [safeguards, and transparency](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/ai-technology-and-innovation/artificial-intelligence/ai_business/) in place.
- **It runs unattended**, sending, updating, or deciding without a person approving each output.
- **It is customer-facing**, answering enquiries, booking, or replying on your behalf.
- **You have tried twice and stalled.** Two stalled attempts usually means the problem is design, not effort.

## How do you decide between DIY and hiring?

| Situation | Do it yourself | Hire help |
|---|---|---|
| Agent works inside one tool you already use | Yes | Only if the tool's own setup is beyond your team |
| Agent connects two or more systems | Possible if you are technical and it is internal | Yes, especially if either system holds client data |
| Output is reviewed by a person before anyone external sees it | Yes | Not needed for this reason alone |
| Output goes to clients or customers without review | No | Yes |
| Touches customer or client personal information | Only for your own records, with care | Yes |
| You can name the workflow in one sentence | Yes | Yes, and you will get a better quote |
| You cannot name the workflow yet | Map your repeated tasks first | Not yet. A short diagnostic, not a build |
| You have stalled twice on your own | Stop | Yes |

## What does DIY really cost in founder time?

DIY is free in dollars and expensive in hours. Getting a demo working is usually quick. Most of the cost comes after that:

- **Edge cases.** The agent handles the typical case on day one. The unusual 20 percent of inputs take most of the effort.
- **Maintenance.** Tools change their connectors, models change behaviour, and someone has to notice and fix it.
- **Context switching.** Every hour the founder spends debugging an automation is an hour not spent selling or delivering.

A simple way to decide: estimate the hours you will spend getting it reliable and multiply by what your time is worth. Compare that with a fixed-price quote. Published Canadian ranges for a single-workflow build start around [$500 to $3,000](https://planwebbies.ca/blog/ai-automation-cost-canada/), and most small-business agent projects land between [$5,000 and $15,000](https://chatgpt.ca/blog/ai-consulting-rate-card-canada-2026). ## What should you do before hiring anyone?

Five things make any provider faster, cheaper, and easier to judge:

1. **Name the workflow in one sentence.**
2. **Measure it today.** How often it runs and how long it takes.
3. **Collect 20 to 30 real past examples** of the work, good and bad.
4. **List the tools involved** and who holds admin access to each.
5. **Name an owner** on your team who will review the agent's work.
