**A single, narrow AI agent usually takes 2 to 6 weeks from kickoff to daily use.** Agent features built into a tool you already use can be live within days, while multi-agent systems or agents integrated deeply across many tools typically take 2 to 6 months.

The build itself is rarely the slow part. Access to tools, gathering real examples, and testing on edge cases take most of the time, so the best way to shorten a project is to prepare those before it starts.

*Updated 2026-09-24 by Esteban Valencia, EV Advisory. Written for founder-led businesses in Canada and the United States.*

## How long does each type of AI agent take?

| Agent type | Typical timeline | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| Built-in agent feature in a tool you already use | A few days | Configuration and testing inside one tool |
| Single-workflow agent, internal, one or two tools | 2 to 4 weeks | Access, examples, and a week or two of testing alongside your team |
| Single-workflow agent, customer-facing or touching personal information | 4 to 6 weeks | More testing, guardrails, escalation paths, and privacy review |
| Multi-workflow implementation across several tools | 6 to 12 weeks | Each integration adds build, testing, and handoff |
| Multi-agent or organization-wide system | 2 to 6 months or more | Coordination between agents, governance, and change management across teams |

Published Canadian sources are consistent with this: most small-business agent projects are scoped as [2 to 4 week fixed-price engagements](https://chatgpt.ca/blog/ai-consulting-rate-card-canada-2026).

## What happens week by week?

For a typical single-workflow agent:

- **Before week 1: scope.** The workflow, tools, success measure, and price are agreed in writing.
- **Week 1: access and examples.** Tool access is granted, 20 to 30 real past examples are collected, and the current process is measured as a baseline.
- **Weeks 2 to 3: build.** The agent is built inside your accounts and connected to your tools.
- **Weeks 3 to 4: test on real work.** The agent runs on past examples, then alongside your team on live work, with a person approving every output.
- **Week 4 or 5: launch and handoff.** The agent moves into daily use; your owner gets the runbook and a walkthrough.
- **Following 2 to 4 weeks: early monitoring.** Exceptions are watched and the agent is tuned.

## What slows an implementation down?

- **Waiting for access.** The single most common delay. Admin rights to a CRM or inbox often sit with one busy person.
- **No real examples.** Testing on made-up cases hides the problems that matter.
- **A workflow that is not written down.** If the process lives in someone's head, it has to be discovered first.
- **Scope creep.** "While you are in there, can it also..." Each addition restarts part of the testing.
- **Slow reviews.** If test outputs sit unreviewed for a week, the project loses a week.
- **Unclear ownership.** Nobody on your side is responsible for saying "this is good enough".

## How can you speed it up?

1. Name one process owner before kickoff.
2. Grant tool access on day one, scoped to what the agent needs.
3. Have 20 to 30 real past examples ready.
4. Book the review checkpoints in calendars up front.
5. Keep the first agent to one workflow. Add the second once the first is running.
