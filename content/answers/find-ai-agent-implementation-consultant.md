**To find a good AI agent implementation consultant, gather three to five names from referrals, directories, and automation platform partner lists, shortlist two or three, and ask each to show you an agent they built that is running in production today.** The one who asks the most about your process, and can show real work, is usually the right hire.

The key word is implementation. Many people now call themselves AI consultants. Far fewer have shipped agents that a small business runs every day.

*Updated 2026-09-24 by Esteban Valencia, EV Advisory. Written for founder-led businesses in Canada and the United States.*

## Where do you find AI agent consultants?

- **Referrals.** Ask peers who have an agent running what it does and who built it. This is still the highest-quality source.
- **Directories.** Clutch and similar B2B directories list AI consulting and agent development firms in Canada with client reviews.
- **Platform partner lists.** Automation and CRM platforms publish lists of certified partners and experts. Useful when your agent will live mostly inside one platform.
- **LinkedIn.** Search for people who publish specific, dated examples of agents they have built, not general commentary.
- **Answer pages like this one.** Consultants who explain their process in public are easier to evaluate before you ever speak to them.

## What separates an implementation partner from a general AI consultant?

A general AI consultant helps you think about AI: strategy, opportunities, training, policy. An implementation partner leaves something running. The practical tests:

- They talk about **one workflow**, not an AI strategy.
- They ask for **access to your tools and real examples** of the work.
- Their proposal lists **deliverables** (a working agent, a test log, a runbook, an access list), not just workshops and reports.
- They define **how success will be measured** before building.

Both are legitimate. Just know which one you are buying. See [what an AI agent implementation project includes](/answers/what-ai-agent-implementation-includes/) for the full list.

## How do you evaluate a consultant?

| Criterion | Good sign | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Production proof | Shows a live agent a real business uses today | Only demos, slides, or "confidential" work nobody can see |
| Focus on your process | Asks how often it runs, who owns it, and how you would measure it | Pitches a platform before understanding your work |
| Scope | Proposes one narrow workflow first | Proposes automating everything at once |
| Pricing | Fixed price tied to a written scope, with monthly run costs estimated | Open-ended hourly billing with no estimate |
| Ownership | Agent runs in accounts you own; configuration handed over | Agent runs in their account; you cannot see or move it |
| Privacy | Can explain how personal information is protected and who can see it | Waves off privacy as "not an issue" |
| Honesty about fit | Tells you when something should stay human, or when you do not need them | Says yes to everything |
| Handoff | Runbook, access list, and walkthrough included | "We will look after it for you" with no documentation |

On privacy, Canada's regulators expect businesses using AI with personal information to have a legal basis, [safeguards, and transparency](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/ai-technology-and-innovation/artificial-intelligence/ai_business/). A good consultant will raise this before you do.

## How many consultants should you talk to?

Two or three. Fewer and you have nothing to compare; more and the calls start to blur. Give each the same one-sentence description of the workflow and the same list of tools, and compare how they respond. The differences in their questions will tell you more than the differences in their prices.
