**Buy first. Most small businesses should start with an off-the-shelf or built-in AI agent, run it on real work for 30 to 60 days, and build a custom agent only where their process is different enough that a generic tool gets in the way.**

Building makes sense when the agent needs to connect several of your tools in a way no product does, when the workflow is part of what makes your business different, or when a bought tool's cost at your volume is higher than a custom build.

*Updated 2026-09-24 by Esteban Valencia, EV Advisory. Written for founder-led businesses in Canada and the United States.*

## When should you buy an off-the-shelf agent?

Buy when:

- **The task is common.** Answering standard support questions, booking meetings, summarising calls, and drafting emails are solved problems with good products.
- **The agent lives inside one tool.** Many CRMs, help desks, and project tools now ship agent features you can switch on.
- **You want to learn fast.** A bought agent teaches you what works on your data before you spend on a build.
- **You have no one to maintain a custom build.**

## When should you build a custom agent?

Build, or have one built, when:

- **The workflow spans several tools** that no single product connects well, for example project tool to CRM to shared drive.
- **The way you do it is part of your value.** Your onboarding, your reporting format, or your qualification rules are what clients pay for.
- **The bought tool is forcing workarounds** that cost more time than the tool saves.
- **Per-seat or per-task pricing at your volume** is higher than a custom build plus run cost over 12 months.
- **You need control over where data goes**, for example to meet a client's requirements about personal information.

## How do you decide?

| Factor | Buy | Build |
|---|---|---|
| Task is common across businesses | Yes | Rarely worth it |
| Agent lives inside one tool | Yes | No |
| Workflow spans three or more tools | Sometimes, with connectors | Usually |
| Process is a differentiator | No | Yes |
| You need results in days | Yes | No, expect weeks |
| You need control over data flows | Only if the vendor's terms fit | Yes |
| You have someone to maintain it | Not required | Required, in-house or on a support arrangement |
| 12-month cost at your volume | Lower at low volume | Can be lower at high volume |

## What does a hybrid path look like?

Most small businesses end up here:

1. **Buy** a built-in or off-the-shelf agent for the most common task.
2. **Pilot** it on real work for 30 to 60 days, with a person reviewing outputs.
3. **Measure** time saved, error rate, and where it falls short.
4. **Build** a narrow custom agent only for the gap the bought tool cannot close, connecting it to the tools you already use.

Whichever path you take, check how the tool or builder handles personal information. Canada's privacy regulators set out [principles for responsible generative AI](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/) that apply to organizations that use these tools, not only those that build them.
