**Today an AI agent can reliably handle the repetitive, rules-based work around a service business's clients: lead intake and first replies, scheduling, call and meeting summaries, CRM updates, follow-ups, intake forms, review requests, routine support questions, and first drafts of documents.** It still needs a person for pricing, commitments, complaints, sensitive conversations, and any decision with real consequences.

The practical line is not what an agent can attempt; it is what it can do reliably enough that reviewing its work takes less time than doing the work.

*Updated 2026-09-24 by Esteban Valencia, EV Advisory. Written for founder-led businesses in Canada and the United States.*

## Which tasks are agents reliable at today?

| Task | What the agent handles | What a human keeps |
|---|---|---|
| Lead intake and first reply | Reads the enquiry, checks fit, drafts a reply, logs the lead | Approving the reply, qualifying calls, pricing |
| Scheduling | Offers times, books, reschedules, sends reminders | Exceptions and priority clients |
| Phone answering | Answers common questions, captures details, books appointments | Upset callers, complex requests, anything sensitive |
| Call and meeting summaries | Summarises, lists decisions, creates tasks | Confirming owners and commitments |
| CRM updates | Creates contacts, logs activity, updates deal stages | Deal strategy and forecasts |
| Follow-ups | Drafts follow-ups after calls, quotes, and silence | Tone with key accounts |
| Client intake and onboarding | Sends forms, chases missing items, sets up folders and projects | The kickoff conversation |
| Review requests | Asks happy clients for reviews at the right moment | Responding to negative reviews |
| Routine support questions | Answers from your own policies and documents | Complaints, refunds, exceptions |
| First drafts | Status updates, reports, proposals from templates, briefs | Final content and anything client-facing |
| Invoice reminders | Drafts polite overdue reminders | Any conversation about money |

## Which tasks still need a human?

- **Pricing, quotes, and commitments.** An agent can prepare the numbers; a person commits the business.
- **Complaints and upset clients.** People want to be heard by a person.
- **Hiring, firing, and anything about staff.**
- **Legal, medical, or financial advice** to clients.
- **Decisions using sensitive personal information.** Canada's privacy regulators expect businesses using AI with personal information to have a legal basis for the data, apply [safeguards, and be transparent](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/ai-technology-and-innovation/artificial-intelligence/ai_business/), including when AI made a decision.
- **Anything new.** The first time a situation comes up, a person should handle it and decide whether the agent should handle it next time.

## What does a handoff between the agent and a person look like?

A good handoff has four parts:

1. **A clear trigger.** The agent knows when to stop: low confidence, an upset tone, a request outside its scope, or a keyword like "refund" or "cancel".
2. **A named person.** The case goes to someone specific, not a shared inbox nobody watches.
3. **A summary.** The person gets what the agent found and what it was about to do, so they do not start from zero.
4. **A response time.** Everyone knows how quickly a human will pick it up, and the client is told.

Test the handoff as carefully as the agent itself. Most agent failures clients notice are handoff failures.

## Which industries use AI most today?

Among Canadian businesses, adoption is highest in information and cultural industries (42.3%), finance and insurance (40.4%), and professional, scientific and technical services (32.4%), according to [Statistics Canada's second-quarter 2026 analysis](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm). Service businesses are among the heaviest users.
