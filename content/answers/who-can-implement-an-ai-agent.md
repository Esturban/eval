**In Canada, AI agents are implemented by five kinds of providers: your own team using built-in tools, freelancers, senior-led consultants, specialist AI agencies, and large consultancies. For a small business's first agent, a senior-led consultant or an experienced specialist freelancer is usually the best fit, because the job is small enough for one person and the hardest part is choosing the right workflow.**

Large consultancies are built for enterprise programs. Agencies make sense once you need several agents or managed support. The table below sorts out which provider type fits which situation.

*Updated 2026-09-24 by Esteban Valencia, EV Advisory. Written for founder-led businesses in Canada and the United States.*

## What kinds of providers implement AI agents?

| Provider type | Best for | Typical engagement |
|---|---|---|
| Your own team, using agent features built into your tools | Simple, internal tasks inside one tool | Hours to a few days of setup; subscription cost only |
| Freelancer | A clearly specified build you have already scoped | Fixed price or hourly, days to a few weeks |
| Senior-led consultant or boutique | Choosing and scoping the first agent, then building or overseeing it | Fixed-price project, often 2 to 6 weeks |
| Specialist AI agency or dev shop | Several agents, larger integrations, managed support | Multi-month projects and monthly retainers |
| Large consultancy | Enterprise programs across departments, with governance and change management | Multi-month to multi-year programs |

For published Canadian rates by provider type, see [freelancer vs agency vs consultant](/answers/freelancer-vs-agency-vs-consultant-ai-agents/) and the [AI agent cost guide](/answers/ai-agent-implementation-cost/).

## How do you choose between them?

Answer three questions:

1. **Do you already know exactly which workflow to automate?** If yes, a freelancer or agency can build to your spec. If no, start with someone whose job is to help you choose, usually a senior-led consultant.
2. **How many agents do you need in the next year?** One or two: a consultant or freelancer. Several, across teams: an agency.
3. **Does the agent touch client or customer personal information?** If yes, favour a provider who can explain how they apply [Canadian privacy expectations for AI](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/ai-technology-and-innovation/artificial-intelligence/ai_business/), including safeguards and transparency.

## What proof should a provider show?

Ask for agents running in production, not demos. Specifically:

- **A live agent** they built, doing real work for a real business, with the owner's permission to show it.
- **A before-and-after measure** for that agent: time saved, volume handled, or error rate.
- **A sample runbook or handoff document**, so you can see what you would receive.
- **Where the agent runs.** In the client's own accounts, or the provider's? You want yours.

If a provider only has slides and demos, you would be their test case. That can be fine at the right price, but it should be said plainly.

## How common is it for Canadian businesses to use AI now?

More common every quarter. Statistics Canada reported that [19.2% of Canadian businesses used AI](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm) to produce goods or deliver services in the 12 months before the second quarter of 2026, roughly triple the 6.1% reported two years earlier. The federal [SME AI adoption blueprint](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en/sme-ai-adoption-blueprint) notes that smaller firms adopt more slowly than large ones because they face distinct barriers, which is why the choice of provider matters.
