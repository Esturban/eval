**For most small service businesses, the first thing to automate with AI is inbound lead intake and first response: an agent reads each new enquiry, checks it against your fit criteria, drafts a reply, and logs it in your CRM for a person to review and send.** It runs daily, follows clear rules, is easy to measure (time to first reply), and nothing reaches a prospect without a person approving it.

If lead intake is not a problem for you, use the five criteria below to pick a different first workflow. The rule behind them: start with work that is frequent, predictable, reviewed before it leaves the business, and measurable today.

*Updated 2026-09-24 by Esteban Valencia, EV Advisory. Written for founder-led businesses in Canada and the United States.*

## How do you pick the first process to automate?

Score each candidate against five criteria. The first automation should pass all five.

| Criterion | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Frequent | Runs at least weekly, ideally daily. Monthly tasks rarely repay the setup |
| Predictable | Follows rules you could write down. Few genuine judgment calls |
| Low risk | A mistake is caught by a person before it reaches a client, customer, or the public |
| Inputs in a tool | The information the agent needs already lives in an inbox, form, CRM, or project tool |
| Measurable today | You know roughly how long it takes and how often it runs, so you can prove the result |

The one-page [Narrow Agent Fit Checklist](/get/narrow-agent-fit-checklist/) turns this into six yes-or-no checks you can run against any process.

## What are good first candidates?

- **Inbound lead intake and first response.** The default recommendation above.
- **Meeting notes to tasks.** Turn call transcripts into summaries, decisions, and assigned tasks in your project tool.
- **Weekly client status updates.** Draft each client's update from the project tool for an account manager to send. The best first pick for most [small agencies](/answers/best-first-ai-agent-for-small-agency/).
- **New client onboarding intake.** Send forms, chase missing items, and set up folders and projects.
- **Invoice and payment reminders.** Draft polite follow-ups on overdue invoices for someone to approve.
- **Internal questions.** Answer staff questions from your own policies and process documents.

## What should you not automate first?

- **Anything that goes to customers unreviewed.** Earn that level of trust over weeks, not on day one.
- **Pricing, proposals, hiring, and financial decisions.** High stakes and context no tool holds.
- **A process nobody owns.** An agent will not fix an unowned process; it will make the gaps faster.
- **Anything using sensitive personal information** before you have checked your obligations. Canada's privacy regulators expect [safeguards and transparency](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/ai-technology-and-innovation/artificial-intelligence/ai_business/) when AI tools use personal information.
- **Work that happens a few times a year.** Not enough repetition to pay back the setup.

## How do you know it worked?

Measure the process for a week or two before the automation goes live, then again after. For lead intake, track:

- **Time to first reply**, before and after.
- **Hours per week** spent on intake.
- **Correction rate:** how often a person rewrites the drafted reply.
- **Leads logged correctly** in the CRM.

If time to first reply drops and the correction rate falls over the first month, it worked. The [AI agent ROI guide](/answers/measure-ai-agent-roi/) covers how to turn that into a dollar figure.

## Are other small businesses doing this yet?

Yes. Statistics Canada reported that in the second quarter of 2026, [19.2% of Canadian businesses used AI](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm) to produce goods or deliver services, up from 6.1% two years earlier, and 19.9% of businesses with 1 to 4 employees did.
